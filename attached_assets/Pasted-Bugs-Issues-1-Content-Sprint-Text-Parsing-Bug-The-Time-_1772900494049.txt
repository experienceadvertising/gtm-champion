Bugs / Issues
1. Content Sprint Text Parsing Bug — The "Time-to-Inbox Benchmark" sprint has garbled steps: step 3 cuts off mid-word ("box plots, p"), step 4 is just the number "9", and step 5 starts with "and annotate with setup details." The AI output is being split incorrectly into numbered steps.
2. Onboarding Tour Popups Get Clipped — On normal viewport sizes, tour steps 3 and 6 have their Next/Back buttons cut off below the screen. I had to resize the browser window to advance. The popups should reposition or scroll within the viewport.
3. Weekly Focus Shows 9 Tasks but "0 of 13 Completed" — The High-Impact Tasks section lists 9 items under "New," but the Weekly Focus widget references 13. The mismatch is confusing — users don't know where the other 4 are.
4. LLMs/AEO Channel Page Has a Big Dead Zone — There's a large block of white space between the "Why This Matters" blurb and the actual strategy content below. Other channel pages don't have this problem.
5. Pricing Card Is Nearly Invisible — The feature list on the pricing card uses extremely light purple text on a white background. It's hard to read and likely fails accessibility contrast standards.
Suggestions to Make Generated Content More Personalized
1. Pull in the company's actual product names, features, and pricing tiers — Right now the strategies reference "Postmark" by name, but the tactics are somewhat generic (e.g., "run competitor campaigns for 'SendGrid alternative'"). If the scraper extracted Postmark's specific features (message streams, <1s inbox speed, no dedicated IPs), pricing ($15/mo for 10k emails), and product terminology, the strategies could reference those exact details instead of paraphrasing broadly.
2. Detect the company's existing content and channels, then fill gaps — The tool should crawl beyond the homepage: check if the company has a blog, YouTube channel, active LinkedIn, existing case studies, a docs site, etc. Then the strategy should say "You already have 42 blog posts but zero comparison pages — prioritize competitor landing pages" instead of recommending everything from scratch as if it's all net-new.
3. Identify the company's actual competitors from the site and reviews — Instead of guessing competitors (SendGrid, Mailgun), scrape the site for "vs" pages, G2/Capterra reviews, or pricing page comparisons to know exactly who the company already positions against. Then tailor paid search and content strategies around those specific rivals with real differentiation points.
4. Use the ICP score more deeply — The dashboard shows "ICP Score: 85/100" but doesn't explain what that means or how it shapes the strategies. Show the detected ICP (e.g., "Mid-market SaaS dev teams, 11–1,000 employees") and let users confirm or correct it. Then have every strategy explicitly reference that ICP — ad targeting, content tone, channel selection should all visibly tie back to who the customer actually is.
5. Tailor the Content Tools outputs to the company's voice — The LinkedIn Post Generator and Blog Writer don't seem to analyze the company's existing tone, writing style, or brand voice from the website copy. Scrape 3–5 pages of their site copy, detect whether they're technical/developer-focused, casual, enterprise-formal, etc., and match the generated content to that voice instead of defaulting to a generic tone.
6. Reference real customer proof from the site — Postmark's homepage has specific testimonials ("11/10 would recommend," the Growform migration story). The Content Tools and strategy recommendations should pull in those actual quotes and customer names for social proof in generated LinkedIn posts, email campaigns, and ad copy — rather than using placeholder suggestions like "include customer tweets."
7. Factor in the company's GTM motion — The tool correctly tagged Postmark as "PLG + Sales Hybrid," but the strategies don't clearly differentiate which tactics serve the self-serve PLG funnel vs. the sales-assisted motion. Split recommendations: "For your PLG funnel: optimize docs SEO and in-app onboarding emails. For your sales motion: ABM microsites and SDR sequences." This makes the output dramatically more actionable.
8. Make the Weekly Content Sprints reactive to what's trending in their space — The content sprint ideas are solid but static. If the tool monitored recent news, product launches, or trending topics in the email deliverability / transactional email space (e.g., Google's new sender requirements), the weekly sprints could suggest timely content like "Google just announced X — here's a blog post angle and LinkedIn post to publish this week."
